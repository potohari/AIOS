# The Three Ms and the Four Cs

## Four Cs (build in this order; each depends on the one before)
| Pillar | Meaning | Quick test | Pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context | What the AI knows: owner, team, offer, voice, priorities | Fresh session: "What should I focus on this week?" | Answers from real priorities |
| Connections | Tools/data it can reach | "What is on my plate today?" | Pulls live tasks, calendar, messages |
| Capabilities | Skills built from the owner's procedures | One-line request for a routine deliverable | Follows the procedure, little back-and-forth |
| Cadence | Acts by itself on a schedule | "What ran since yesterday?" | Scheduled runs happened and reported |

## Three Ms
- **Mindset.** Default shift: before any task ask how much AI can do (rarely 0% or 100%; 30% is a win). Function breakdown: every role is a tree of small tasks; automate one chunk at a time and reuse it. Curiosity rule: never accept output without asking why it was built that way and what happens in odd cases.
- **Method.** Decide what is worth automating and how far. Prove with the cheapest version first. Prefer simple fixed workflows over autonomous agents when steps never change ("boring is beautiful").
- **Machine.** Files, skills, connections, schedules.

## Seven tier-one connection domains
Revenue, Customer, Calendar, Comms, Tasks, Meetings, Knowledge.
Readiness test: could an agent holding all these answer most questions the team or customers bring to the owner?

## Operating rhythm
- Every morning: "Help me plan my day." If weak, fix the missing connection/priority/context.
- End of day: which skills ran, what was corrected, what was copy-pasted. Patch one thing.
- Every Friday: /audit and save; schedule skills used daily; /level-up if next step unclear.
- Each quarter/sprint: refresh context/priorities.md, prune CLAUDE.md.
